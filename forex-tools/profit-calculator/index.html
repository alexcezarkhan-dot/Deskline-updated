What is DeskTerminal's Profit Calculator?

This calculator works out the exact profit or loss on a forex trade from your entry and exit price, before or after you actually place it. Enter both prices, your lot size, and which direction you traded, and it handles the currency conversion itself — including cross pairs that don't involve USD at all, like EUR/GBP or AUD/JPY, using live exchange rates rather than a static assumption.

Add your broker's fee per trade and the result splits into gross and net profit, so you can see the real number that actually lands in your account, not just the raw price movement.

How to use this calculator

Fill in six fields, and the result updates as you go:

  • Instrument — the pair, metal, or crypto you're trading
  • Deposit currency — the currency your trading account is actually funded in
  • Direction — buy (long) or sell (short)
  • Open price — where you entered, or plan to enter
  • Close price — where you exited, or where you're testing a hypothetical exit
  • Trade size — your position size in lots

Behind the scenes, it takes the difference between your two prices and multiplies it by your position size and the instrument's real contract value — the same underlying math as the pip calculator, just carried through to a full profit figure instead of stopping at a single pip's worth.

How forex profit actually works

A buy (long) position profits when the price rises after you enter — you're buying low with the intent to sell higher later. A sell (short) position works the opposite way: you're effectively selling first, aiming to buy back at a lower price than you sold at.

A simple illustration: buying 1 standard lot of gold at $1,890.00 and closing at $1,891.00 — a $1.00 move — on a 100-unit contract works out to $100 profit. The same $1.00 move against a sell position would be a $100 loss instead, since the direction is reversed. This calculator handles that direction logic automatically based on which one you select, rather than requiring you to work out the sign yourself.

Once you're comfortable with the profit side, the position size calculator flips the question around — instead of "what will this trade earn," it answers "how large should this trade be," based on how much of your account you're willing to risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate forex profit or loss?

Enter your entry price, exit price, trade size, and direction (buy or sell) — the calculator works out your exact gross and net profit using live exchange rates for accurate cross-pair conversion.

Does this handle cross currency pairs correctly?

Yes — the calculator automatically converts profit/loss into your deposit currency using live exchange rates, correctly handling cross pairs that don't involve USD directly, such as EUR/GBP or AUD/JPY.

Can I include my broker's trading fees?

Yes — enter your broker's actual fee per trade, and the calculator shows both gross profit and net profit after fees.

What's the difference between gross and net profit here?

Gross is the raw result of the price move alone. Net subtracts whatever fee you entered, giving the number that actually reflects what hits your account after costs.

Can I calculate a loss, not just a profit?

Yes — if your exit price moved against your position, the result shows as a negative number in both gross and net, exactly like a real losing trade would.

Does this work for gold and crypto, or only forex pairs?

Gold, silver, and major cryptocurrencies are all supported alongside forex, each using its own real contract size and live price.

Can I use this before placing a trade, to plan my risk?

Yes — entering a hypothetical exit price at your planned take-profit or stop-loss level shows what that outcome would actually be worth, which is often more useful than checking after the fact.

Does the deposit currency I select actually change the result?

Yes — switch it and the gross, fees, and net figures all recalculate immediately in that currency, using the current live rate.

Calculations are estimates using standard contract sizes and live exchange rates — always confirm exact contract specs, spreads, and fees with your own broker. Not financial advice.
